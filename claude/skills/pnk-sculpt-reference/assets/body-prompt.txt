Create a single reference image of the following character, to be used as input
to a photogrammetry-style 3D reconstruction. Follow the technical requirements
exactly; they matter more than artistic flair.

CHARACTER
{CHARACTER}

RENDER STYLE
A 3D RENDER of a stylized video-game character model, as if a finished model
were rendered in Blender or ZBrush. Smooth sculpted forms with clear volume.
Matte, slightly rough surfaces. NOT an illustration, NOT concept art, NOT cel
shaded, NO ink outlines, NO painterly brushwork, NO flat colour fills.

LIGHTING
Even, soft, diffuse studio light from the front and slightly above, with gentle
wraparound fill so that every surface is legible. No hard rim light, no strong
specular highlights, no coloured gels, no dramatic shadows, no glow or bloom.

POSE
Relaxed A-pose, facing the viewer straight on, standing upright, weight even on
both feet. Arms held clearly away from the torso with a wide, obvious gap of
empty background visible between each arm and the body. Legs slightly apart with
background visible between them. Both hands empty and open, fingers relaxed and
slightly separated.

FRAMING
The complete figure from the top of the head to below the feet, entirely inside
the frame with a margin of empty space on all four sides. Nothing cropped by the
edge. The figure fills most of the frame vertically.

BACKGROUND
Plain, flat, mid-grey, completely empty. No floor, no ground plane, no cast
shadow, no contact shadow, no environment, no props, no text, no watermark.

FORM REQUIREMENTS
Hair as simple solid sculpted masses and lobes, never individual strands or
wisps. Cloth as thick, solid, sculpted folds, never thin flapping fabric. No
loose straps, ribbons, tassels, chains, cords or strings anywhere. No held
weapons, no held tools, no held objects of any kind. Eyes wide open with clearly
readable eyelids and a defined brow. Every feature carved as real three
dimensional relief rather than painted on.

WHY THESE RULES
The reconstructor infers depth from shading, so flat or outlined art gives it
almost nothing to work with. It resolves volume, so anything thinner than a few
millimetres at final scale comes out broken or missing. It cannot see through
the figure, so limbs touching the torso fuse into one mass. And it invents
whatever the single view does not show, so a hard crop at the frame edge becomes
an invented flat wall.
