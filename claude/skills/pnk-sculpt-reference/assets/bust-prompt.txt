Create a reference image of a BUST SCULPTURE of the character shown in the
attached image, to be used as input to a 3D reconstruction. Keep the same
character: the same face, the same hairstyle, the same colours, the same
headwear and jewellery. Follow the technical requirements exactly.

SUBJECT
A complete, self-contained bust sculpture: head, neck, and the tops of the
shoulders, resting on a simple round plinth. The whole sculpture including the
plinth sits fully inside the frame with empty margin all around. This is a
finished object photographed whole, not a portrait cropped at the chest.

RENDER STYLE
A 3D RENDER of a stylized sculpted bust, smooth clean sculpted surfaces, matte
finish, gentle rounded forms. NOT photoreal, NOT an illustration, NO cel
shading, NO ink outlines, NO skin pores, NO fine surface texture, NO fabric
weave.

LIGHTING
Even, soft, diffuse light from the front and slightly above. No hard shadows, no
strong specular highlights, no rim light, no coloured light.

VIEW
Facing the viewer straight on, head upright and level, chin neither raised nor
lowered. The head fills most of the frame height.

FORM REQUIREMENTS
Hair as a few large smooth sculpted masses with simple carved parting lines,
never individual strands, never fine flyaway hairs. Eyes WIDE OPEN with clearly
sculpted upper and lower lids, a defined brow ridge and a rounded eyeball form
that reads as an actual sphere in the socket. Ears clearly separated from the
hair and the skull with visible background or shadowed gap around them. Any
earrings or ornaments modelled as thick solid forms.

BACKGROUND
Plain, flat, mid-grey, completely empty. No environment, no cast shadow, no
text.

WHY THESE RULES
This bust exists because in a full-body reference the head is only a few percent
of the frame, so after the reconstructor downsamples its input the face has
almost no pixels left and comes out as a blank mask. Reconstructing the head on
its own gives the face the entire frame.

Photoreal detail is actively harmful here: at bust scale, individual hair
strands and skin texture land at voxel scale and the reconstructor turns them
into tens of thousands of floating fragments rather than a surface. Smooth
sculpted form reconstructs cleanly; a beautiful photoreal portrait does not.

The plinth exists because a bust cropped by the frame edge forces the
reconstructor to invent geometry along a hard straight line, which it does
badly. A complete object with a visible base has no such edge.
